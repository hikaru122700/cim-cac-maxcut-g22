## One-paragraph thesis (the spine every section returns to)

The CIM solves MAX-CUT by annealing a network of optical parametric oscillators; the **pump-power schedule `p(t)`** governs the bifurcation that maps spins to a cut, yet it is almost universally set to a hand-tuned linear or sigmoid ramp. **DiPS** asks whether *optimizing the shape of `p(t)`* — including high-dimensional, spectral-conditioned, and gradient-learned schedules — yields better cuts than a near-trivial scalar ramp, **under a matched compute budget and on real G-set instances**. The honest, falsifiable claim the paper defends is a **decomposition of the schedule-optimization lever**: low-dimensional schedule tuning captures most of the available gain, freeform/learned schedules do **not** reliably beat it once budget is matched, and the dominant remaining lever is **feedback control of the dynamics (CAC)** rather than the open-loop pump *shape*. The contribution is the differentiable framework + the compute-matched benchmark that establishes this cleanly.
