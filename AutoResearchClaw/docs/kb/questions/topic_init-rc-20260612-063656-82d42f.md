# Stage 01: Topic Init

# Research Goal: Differentiable Optimization of the CIM Pump-Power Schedule

## Topic
**Coherent Ising Machine (CIM) のポンプ電力関数 *p(t)* の最適化** — i.e., learning the time-dependent pump-rate / gain schedule that drives the degenerate optical parametric oscillator (DOPO) network of a (simulated) Coherent Ising Machine when solving MAX-CUT / Ising ground-state problems. We treat the pump schedule as a *learnable control function* and frame its optimization as a machine-learning problem (amortized control via differentiable simulation).

## Novel Angle
**The specific gap.** In essentially all CIM literature and software, the pump-power function is fixed by hand to a **monotonic linear ramp** *p(t) = p₀ + (p_f − p₀)·t/T* (or a small set of scalar parameters: initial gain, final gain, ramp rate). Hyperparameter tuning of CIM/CAC — including the Optuna/Bayesian sweeps already common in practice — optimizes only **a handful of scalars** of an *assumed-shape* schedule. What has **not** been studied is treating *p(t)* as a **free, high-dimensional functional object** (a piecewise / spline / small-MLP-parameterized curve) and optimizing its *entire shape* directly through the machine's own dynamics, and further **conditioning that shape on cheap instance features** (graph spectral statistics) so the schedule adapts per-problem rather than being one-size-fits-all.

**Why this is timely / what recent development creates the opportunity.** Three ML trends converge here:
1. **Differentiable simulation / backprop-through-rollout.** The mean-field CIM (and CAC) dynamics are a system of ODE/SDEs that integrate cheaply and are **fully differentiable** in modern autodiff frameworks. We can therefore compute *∂(cut value)/∂(schedule parameters)* by backpropagating through the simulated trajectory — exactly the technique that has recently transformed learned optimizers, learned diffusion **noise schedules**, and differentiable physics. Nobody has, to our knowledge, applied *backprop-through-the-Ising-machine* to learn the pump curve.
2. **Learned annealing schedules.** The ML community has repeatedly shown that *learned* or *non-monotonic* schedules beat hand-tuned monotonic ones (learned LR schedules, learned SA temperature schedules, learned diffusion β-schedules). CIM pump power is structurally the same kind of object, yet is still set by a linear heuristic — a clear unexplored transfer.
3. **Amortized / instance-conditioned control.** Rather than re-tuning per graph, we learn a tiny network *g_θ(graph features) → schedule*, amortizing the search so a *new* graph gets a tailored schedule in one forward pass.

**Why a reviewer would NOT say "already known."** The well-known results are: (a) CIM works and roughly rivals SA on MAX-CUT; (b) **Chaotic Amplitude Control (CAC)** improves over plain CIM via error-variable *feedback*; (c) scalar hyperparameters matter. Our claim is **orthogonal and falsifiable against the dominant assumption that the linear ramp is near-optimal**: we ask *what is the optimal pump-power functional, and is it monotonic?* CAC modulates *amplitude per-spin via feedback*; it does **not** answer what the *global pump schedule shape* should be, and CAC itself still rides on a hand-set target-amplitude schedule that our method can optimize. The differentiable-simulation route to the schedule, the instance-conditioning, and the explicit test of the monotonicity assumption are each absent from existing CIM work.

**Concrete testable hypotheses.**
- H1: A learned (possibly **non-monotonic**) pump schedule achieves a higher success probability and lower time-to-solution (TTS) than the best linear ramp at matched compute.
- H2: Instance-conditioned schedules *g_θ(features)* generalize to held-out graphs better than a single globally-tuned schedule.
- H3: Applying the learned-schedule idea *on top of CAC* (optimizing CAC's target-amplitude schedule) further improves over stock CAC.

## Scope
A single focused paper:
- One solver family (CIM mean-field + CAC variant), simulated on GPU.
- One task (MAX-CUT) on one standard benchmark family (G-set), plus a small synthetic generalization set.
- Three schedule parameterizations of increasing expressiveness (linear baseline → piecewise/spline → instance-conditioned MLP).
- The deliverable is the **method + empirical evidence on the monotonicity question and generalization**, *not* an attempt to beat global SOTA cut values.

## SMART Goal
**Specific:** Implement a GPU, batched, *differentiable* mean-field CIM/CAC simulator; parameterize the pump-power function *p(t)* as (i) a linear ramp (baseline), (ii) a free K-knot spline (K ≈ 8–16), and (iii) an instance-conditioned MLP *g_θ(spectral features)*; optimize (ii) and (iii) by backprop-through-rollout (with a straight-through / soft-cut surrogate objective) plus a black-box fallback (CMA-ES/Optuna) for the non-differentiable settling phase.
