## 1. One-paragraph summary

PumpBench, a compute-matched harness that scores eight CIM schedule/solver conditions behind a single rollout-and-score core, ran end-to-end in ~44 s over 8 synthetic graphs at n=3 seeds. The plumbing works; the science does not yet hold. Two metric snapshots disagree qualitatively on identical keys (e.g. `differentiable_freeform_pump` = 0.127 vs 0.348), the numerically dominant arm (CAC, gap ≈ 0.013–0.020) is **off-thesis** because it changes the equations of motion rather than the pump shape, and the actual hypothesis — that high-dimensional / instance-conditioned *p(t)* beats a hand-tuned scalar ramp — is **unsupported and possibly contradicted**, since the trivial four-scalar `optuna_scalar_sigmoid_ramp` (~0.13) is the best in-thesis schedule. A 44 s global compute budget confounds the negative result on high-capacity arms with simple undertraining, so the central bet is *not yet* refuted — only unevidenced. The retrospective below records what to keep, what to fix, and what to run next.

---

## 2. What we set out to test (pre-registered intent)

- **H1 (shape).** Treating *p(t)* as a free, high-dimensional functional object (spline / MLP knots) and optimizing its entire shape through the differentiable rollout beats the standard hand-set monotonic ramp.
- **H2 (conditioning).** Conditioning the schedule on cheap graph spectral features lets it adapt per-instance and beats a single one-size-fits-all schedule.
- **H3 (gradients).** Backprop-through-the-rollout (differentiable simulation) finds at least as good a schedule as a gradient-free twin on the identical parameterization, more cheaply.

**Pre-registered gates that should have decided these:** evaluation on G15/G22/G35 (not synthetic), TTS at cut ≥ 13200, dt-halving 80%-retention check, shape-null band, ∫p dt matching, ≥15–20 seeds, omnibus + Holm correction, leave-one-graph-out for the conditioning claim.

## 4. Lessons learned

### 4.1 Operational / infrastructure
- **L1 — The pipeline aborts without a live acpx session.** The `code_generation` stage failed at exit 4 (`No acpx session found`) and `iterative_refine` failed twice at exit 1. **Always provision the session first:** `acpx claude sessions new --name researchclaw-cim`, and confirm it is connected before launching the experiment stage. This is the single most common, most cheaply avoidable failure in the prior runs.
- **L2 — Never parse stdout for metrics.** `source: stdout_parsed` is the root cause of the two-snapshot contradiction. Stdout is lossy, reorderable, and silently overwritable. Emit one structured `metrics.json` as the *only* metric channel; treat anything scraped from logs as non-authoritative.
- **L3 — Unprefixed aggregate keys collide.** `primary_metric` ended up holding CAC's value and `best_of_k_optimality_gap` the spectral net's, because unindexed keys were overwritten by the last writer. Namespace every metric by `condition × seed × graph`; forbid bare top-level metric keys.
- **L4 — A 44 s global budget is a smoke test, not an experiment.** High-capacity arms were almost certainly undertrained. Budget must be *per-arm* and *compute-matched* (equal function-evaluation count, or a budget-vs-quality curve), not a single wall-clock cap shared across arms of wildly different cost.
